# Leathercraft basics (reference for the teacher)

Written for Awlchemy. Short, practical facts a beginner needs; the teacher expands on them.

## Leather

- **Vegetable-tanned (veg-tan):** firm, takes tooling, stamping, wet-moulding and dye, burnishes to a smooth edge, develops a patina. The default for wallets, card holders, key fobs, belts, sheaths and most printed patterns.
- **Chrome-tanned:** soft, drapey, water-resistant, colourfast. Good for linings, pouches and garments. Its edges don't burnish; finish them with edge paint, or fold or bind them.
- **Combination-tanned:** a mix of both. Firmer than chrome-tan and softer than veg-tan.
- **Grain:** full grain (the strongest and most honest surface) > top grain (sanded or corrected) > split or suede. Avoid bonded leather.
- **Thickness:** 1 oz ≈ 0.4 mm (1/64 in).
  - Card slots and linings: 0.6–1.0 mm (1.5–2.5 oz).
  - Wallet exteriors: 1.0–1.4 mm (2.5–3.5 oz).
  - Key fobs and small goods: 1.2–1.6 mm (3–4 oz).
  - Bags: 1.4–2.0 mm (3.5–5 oz).
  - Belts and straps: 3–4 mm (8–10 oz).
- Thickness on a hide varies by area. Shoulder is a good beginner choice: even and affordable. Belly is stretchy, so keep it for unimportant parts.

## Cutting

- Mark on the flesh side (or lightly on the grain with a silver pen or an awl).
- Cut against a steel ruler, keeping the blade vertical. Use several light passes rather than one hard one. Change or strop blades often, because a dull blade tears.
- Cut curves slowly and turn the leather rather than your wrist. Round punches give clean small radii.
- Always cut away from your body and your other hand.

## Stitching

- **Saddle stitch:** one thread with a needle at each end, passed through every hole from both sides. If one stitch breaks, it doesn't unravel. Thread length is about 4× the seam length, plus extra for the needles.
- **Holes:** diamond (pricking) chisels give slanted, even holes; a round punch or an awl works too.
  - 3–4 mm spacing for small goods, 4–5 mm for bags.
  - The stitch line usually sits 3–4 mm from the edge. Mark it with a wing divider or groover.
- **Thread:** waxed polyester or linen.
  - 0.45–0.65 mm for small goods, 0.8–1.0 mm for bags and belts.
  - Lock the end by backstitching 2–3 holes. Polyester thread can be melted lightly to finish.
- Punch through all the glued layers at once so the holes line up. Use a poundo board or cutting mat under the leather and a mallet, not a steel hammer, on chisels.

## Gluing

- **Contact cement:** a thin layer on both surfaces, wait until it's tacky, then press together. It can't be repositioned. Ventilate the room.
- Water-based leather glue is milder and allows small adjustments.
- Keep glue clear of the edge you'll burnish and of the stitch holes. Rough up glossy surfaces first.

## Edges

1. Bevel the corners of the edge (size 1–2 bevelers for 1–2 mm leather).
2. Sand flush the layers that were glued together (around 220 grit, then finer).
3. Burnish veg-tan: dampen, or apply gum tragacanth or a burnishing agent, then rub fast with a wood slicker or canvas until it's glossy.
4. For chrome-tan or a painted look, apply edge paint in thin coats, sanding between them.

## Hardware

- **Line 20/24 snaps:** need a setter and an anvil matched to the snap size. Practise on scrap first.
- **Rivets (double-cap):** the post should stick out about 2–3 mm above the stack before setting.
- **Split rings, swivel hooks, D-rings, buckles:** size them to the strap width.

## Finishing

- Dye before assembly where possible. Test on scrap, because colours darken.
- Finish with a balm or wax for a natural look, or an acrylic resolene-type finish for protection.
- Neatsfoot oil darkens veg-tan. Apply sparingly and test first.

## Printing patterns

- Print at 100% / "Actual size". Never choose "Fit to page", which shrinks everything by around 3%.
- Measure the calibration square before cutting.
- Glue the printout to card or plastic for a reusable template.

## Good first projects

Key fob → card holder → simple bifold or long wallet → pouch with a snap → belt → small bag. Each one adds one new skill (straight stitching, then curves, layers, hardware, long cuts, then 3D construction).
